### Mapping Neural Reorganization After Injury

The nervous system has a remarkable capacity to reorganize after injury. We use advanced neuroimaging to characterize how the brain and spinal cord adapt following spinal cord injury and stroke, and to identify the neural signatures that predict functional recovery.

**Approaches:**

- **Functional MRI (fMRI)** — to map changes in brain activation patterns and functional connectivity
- **Diffusion Tensor Imaging (DTI)** — to assess structural integrity of white matter tracts, including the corticospinal tract
- **Neurophysiological Assessments** — to probe the excitability and recruitment strength of motor circuits at the cortical, subcortical, and spinal levels
- **Movement Biomechanics** — to discern between beneficial and maladaptive movement patterns post-injury

Our goal is to identify biomarkers of plasticity that can guide personalized rehabilitation strategies and predict who will benefit most from targeted interventions.

### Enhancing Rehabilitation Through Non-invasive Stimulation

Rehabilitation alone may not fully exploit the nervous system's capacity for recovery. We investigate how non-invasive neuromodulation can be combined with rehabilitation to amplify neuroplasticity and improve functional outcomes beyond what rehabilitation achieves alone.

**Techniques:**

- **Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS)** — to modulate cortical excitability and promote beneficial plasticity of the brain
- **Spinal Cord Stimulation** — to enhance spinal circuit excitability and facilitate motor output below the level of injury
- **Peripheral Nerve Stimulation** — to augment sensory input and prime neural circuits for motor learning during rehabilitation

We design and test stimulation protocols that are paired with task-specific rehabilitation, with the goal of translating these combined approaches into clinical practice for people with spinal cord injury and stroke.
